A22 — PROPAGATION WITH INCREASING DEPTH
First theoretical results and a falsifiable next theorem
22 October 2026

This is a mathematical extension study, not a new training campaign. No
original engine file was changed, no native extension was loaded, and no
cell was replayed. Two scalar root brackets and finite matrix products were
checked independently. Documents and executable checks are in English.

EXECUTIVE FINDINGS

1. The historical narrow Q12 mechanism extends to the existing WIDE finite
   head domain: layers 7 through 18 have a certified upper contraction bound
   Q <= 0.6816852954683713304 < 0.682. This concerns date perturbations for
   the SAME fixed physical gains and parameters. It does not identify
   latencies across different learned devices.

2. The existing boundary date boxes at layers 6 and 18 are disjoint even
   after an arbitrary common time translation. Therefore the stored input
   box cannot simply serve as a recurrent domain for this block. A larger
   or differently shaped invariant domain is needed.

3. There is a precise conditional theorem: an autonomous or periodically
   repeated block with an invariant normalized shape domain and uniform
   contraction has an additive eigenprofile and a common asymptotic delay
   per layer. The proof is given below, including the scalar drift.

4. Mixing alone is insufficient, even for the supplied local physical laws.
   We construct a connected, positive-contact, nominal R extension whose
   future words are preserved, whose normalized dates mix uniformly, and
   whose depth-average delay nevertheless has two subsequential limits.
   Its post-preparation states are admissible to the local equations;
   their reachability by the prescribed 128-pulse training is NOT proved.

5. The two-region test has a clear conditional outcome. Uniform global
   mixing plus a bounded intrinsic block delay spread rules out different
   asymptotic lane speeds within one connected strip. The current finite
   certificate does not yet establish these hypotheses at every depth.

1. WHAT IS BEING EXTENDED

The established finite-network statement has the form

    t[n,j,p] = s[p] + d[n,j].

The learning-coverage dossier supplies a manual source-level bridge under
its explicit assumptions A1–A4. Earlier files with an OPEN entry status are
historical drafts superseded at that stated proof level. This study neither
reopens that finite result nor upgrades it to a proof-assistant certificate.

An 18-layer graph does not itself define a sequence as n tends to infinity.
An extension rule must specify new cells, port parameters, learned states,
training histories, and which lanes remain active. Here the principal target
is a fixed-width strip with the same 12-lane periodic adjacency, all lanes
active after an entrance section, and arbitrarily many downstream layers.

The desired statement is, for each fixed admissible extension,

    d[n,j]/n -> tau, for every considered lane j, with 0 < tau < infinity.

Then v=1/tau is the speed in layers per unit time. A common tau within one
device is distinct from a universal tau across different learned devices,
parameter realizations, preparations, or increasing widths. No universal
device-independent speed is established here.

2. RECOVERING THE SPATIAL CONTRACTION

For a cell at exact READY charge r=0.1, define its first head threshold by

    r + sum_i g_i h_+(F-t_i) = 1,
    h(u) = a/(a-kappa) [exp(-kappa*u)-exp(-a*u)],
    a=log(4), kappa=1/2.

Assume strict, uniformly certified guards: contributing ages lie in (0,1),
masked contacts remain after the head, and the threshold slope is positive.
For fixed gains and fixed physical parameters, implicit differentiation gives

    dF/dt_i = g_i h'(F-t_i) / S,
    S = sum_contributing_i g_i h'(F-t_i) > 0.

Masked entries are zero. These derivatives are nonnegative and sum to one.
Translation of all arrivals translates F by the same amount. The relevant
quantity is osc(x)=max_j x_j-min_j x_j, not an absolute timing norm.

Let L_n be a componentwise rational lower bound for the true row-stochastic
Jacobian J_n, uniform over the head domain at layer n. For a block a..b,

    P = L_b ... L_a,
    beta = min_rows_i,j sum_k min(P[i,k],P[j,k]),
    Q = 1-beta.

Every true Jacobian product dominates P. If beta>0, its oscillation operator
norm is at most Q. Integrate the Jacobian along an admissible straight segment
between two entrance vectors: osc(T(x)-T(y)) <= Q osc(x-y). The segment and
all its intermediate heads must remain in the certified domains. This is a
finite-domain statement, not a license to extrapolate those domains.

Why the segment condition holds for the present finite head boxes: the stored
strict endpoint guards enclose each root for all independently enclosed gains
and parent dates, the active/masked classification stays strict, and induction
keeps every intermediate root in the next stored rectangle. A segment in the
layer-6 entrance rectangle therefore has the required enclosed images. Shared
physical gains are held fixed along that segment, even though the bounds are
uniform over their enclosing boxes.

The historical script certificat_domaine_A15.py applies this mechanism to the
last 12 narrow layers. Its approximately 0.6246369142769641 bound belongs to
that narrow domain. We did not reuse this number for the wide domain.

NEW FINITE WIDE RESULT. The supplied wide future-closure certificate contains
strict positive contributing ages at every head. Using directed 70-digit
arithmetic, finite exponential/logarithm series with explicit tails, rational
downward rounding of each influence to a multiple of 10^-6, and exact rational
matrix products, the new checker obtains for layers 7..18:

    beta >= 0.318314704531628669606070668764404571053184995481266547747784669079875449
    Q    <= 0.681685295468371330393929331235595428946815004518733452252215330920124551.

The exact matrices, fractions, head ages and source hashes are in
results/finite_wide_contraction.json. Some shorter blocks also have positive
overlap; they are listed without claiming repeatability.

This contracts the DIFFERENCE between two date profiles propagated through
the same block. It does not assert osc(T(x)) <= Q osc(x): heterogeneous cells
can generate a nonzero output shape even from synchronous inputs.

BOUNDARY FAILURE. If an output vector y from the layer-18 box could equal an
input vector x from the layer-6 box plus c times the all-ones vector, then

    max_j (y_lower[j]-x_upper[j]) <= c
       <= min_j (y_upper[j]-x_lower[j]).

The checker finds the lower bound larger than the upper bound. Thus these
two boxes have no common shape. This specifically rejects direct recycling
of the stored input rectangle. It does not prove that a larger invariant
shape domain is impossible, nor that repeating a physical block is invalid.

3. A SUFFICIENT THEOREM THAT ALSO CONTROLS THE DRIFT

Theorem (repeated block). Let T map the arrival-date vector across a block
of m layers with fixed learned physical data. On a translation-invariant
domain suppose:

(i) T(x+c*1)=T(x)+c*1, and the physical head map is well defined;
(ii) S is a nonempty closed set of normalized vectors z with z[0]=0;
(iii) H(z)=T(z)-T(z)[0]*1 maps S into itself;
(iv) osc(H(z)-H(w)) <= rho osc(z-w), with rho<1;
(v) lambda(z)=T(z)[0] is Lipschitz on S in the oscillation norm;
(vi) all intermediate layers have bounded relative delays, and uniform
     local head, gain-protection, tail, resource and recovery margins hold.

Then H has a unique fixed shape z*. Write x_k=c_k*1+z_k at block boundaries.
The normalized sequence converges geometrically to z*, while

    c_(k+1)-c_k = lambda(z_k) -> lambda* = lambda(z*).

In fact the Lipschitz condition gives a summable error:

    sum_k |lambda(z_k)-lambda*|
       <= Lip(lambda) osc(z_0-z*)/(1-rho).

Therefore c_k=k*lambda*+O(1), x_k/k -> lambda* times 1, and the bounded
intermediate-layer offsets imply

    d[n,j]/n -> tau = lambda*/m.

If block delays are uniformly positive and finite, tau is positive and finite.

Proof. On the gauge z[0]=0, osc is a norm; closed S is complete. The contraction
mapping theorem gives z*, convergence and its geometric bound. Translation
equivariance gives the scalar recurrence. Summing the Lipschitz errors proves
the O(1) drift remainder. Writing n=km+r, 0<=r<m, finishes the layer claim.
For a monotone differentiable head map with stochastic Jacobian, the scalar
component is Lipschitz on the admitted convex domain, since the infinity norm
of its derivative is one and ||z-w||_infinity <= osc(z-w) on the fixed gauge.

An m-periodic physical extension is autonomous at block boundaries. A merely
depth-dependent extension is not. Alternatively, if normalized block maps
converge uniformly to a common contraction, their drift functions converge
uniformly to a continuous limit, and a common invariant compact shape domain
exists, the same limit conclusion follows: a_k <= rho*a_(k-1)+epsilon_k with
epsilon_k -> 0 gives shape convergence; drift convergence gives the Cesaro
limit. This is another sufficient assumption, not an observed A22 fact.

4. THE TWO-REGION TEST

First distinguish a topological contact from an effective head influence. A
late input can be physically connected but have derivative zero because the
head already fired. Therefore a connected wiring diagram alone is insufficient.

Suppose every consecutive block T_k has contraction rho<1 on the relevant
date domain, the synchronous reference belongs to that domain, and

    osc(T_k(0)) <= B, uniformly in k.

Then, using translation equivariance,

    D_(k+1)=osc(x_(k+1)) <= rho D_k + B,
    D_k <= rho^k D_0 + B/(1-rho).

Hence (x_k[i]-x_k[j])/k -> 0. Two different limiting lane speeds within this
strip are impossible. Their common drift may still fail to converge. If zero
shape is inadmissible, use an admitted reference shape and explicitly bound
the corresponding forcing term; do not silently insert zero into the domain.

A simpler physical obstruction to persistent separation is available in the
all-active-head regime. If all three parent arrivals are strictly between
F-1 and F for every cell, any pair of neighboring parent lanes differs by
less than one time unit because they share a child. On a 12-lane ring this
bounds the spatial diameter by six. Two regions with different linear depth
slopes must eventually leave this regime. With masked contacts the appropriate
late-input/protection bound must replace this argument.

If effective influence products split into two closed components instead,
the abstract recurrence diag(W_A,W_B)x+(a*1_A,b*1_B) permits two speeds a!=b
while preserving translated input words. This is a diagnostic model, NOT a
counterexample using the fixed A22 wiring and reachable learned states. It
identifies the cut whose uniformly positive influence would need proof.

To decide the physical learned-state question, two separately trained regions
must be joined using the actual cross-port couplings and admissible histories.
All first-root, no-extra-fire, error-gate, raw-tail and recovery guards must be
verified at the interface. Persistent separation, if admissible, refutes the
global mixing premise. Loss of those guards shows failure of that candidate
domain, not automatically failure of the physical system or its motifs.

5. A PHYSICAL COUNTEREXAMPLE TO 'MIXING IMPLIES A DEPTH SPEED'

This construction uses the exact nominal local R equations and the same
three-parent 12-lane periodic adjacency. It starts at a downstream interface
with all lanes active and synchronous, after an ASSUMED preparation. It is
not asserted to be the output of the supplied lane-0 training protocol.

At every cell take q=r, auxiliary charges r_m, no pending currents, E in
[50,100], and b=1.1. Choose one of two gain profiles, common to all lanes of
a layer but allowed to vary between layers:

    A: (0.6,1.2,0.6), total gain 2.4;
    B: (0.9,1.2,0.9), total gain 3.0.

Both profiles are strictly positive, below the nominal cap 4, and no smaller
than the nominal initial gains componentwise. These facts are necessary
compatibility checks, not a proof of training attainability.

For synchronous parent arrivals, the local head delay u_G solves

    0.1 + G h(u_G) = 1.

Directed scalar brackets prove

    u_A in (0.388183908205202, 0.388183908205205),
    u_B in (0.281469348037381, 0.281469348037385).

They are distinct by h'>0. The exact rational brackets are supplied in JSON.
Nominal auxiliary marks end at age 0.04, before either firing; their errors
occupy ages [1.10,1.25], entirely inside principal protection. Thus neither
gains nor b changes on future waves. Reopening occurs after a protected
duration 1+log(2.5)/1.1, approximately 1.833. Raw tails have ended and every
cell and auxiliary recovers before the next same-port input with gap >=20.
The existing resource inequality applies to these smaller gains as well.

The construction is robust to a date-shape tube osc(t)<=0.01. Monotonicity
and translation equivariance put every output in [min(t)+u_G,max(t)+u_G],
so this tube maps into itself for either profile. All head ages lie inside
[0.19,0.51]; strict physical margins are checked on this larger interval.
Every one of the three influences is at least 1/20. On the ring, six steps
connect every source lane to every target lane, padding paths by self-edges.
Every entry of the six-step Jacobian product is therefore >=(1/20)^6 and

    Q_6 <= 1-12/20^6 = 15999997/16000000 < 1.

Now alternate A and B in runs of lengths L_k=2^(2^k), k>=1. For exactly
synchronous interface pulses, all lanes stay exactly synchronous and

    d[n,j] = sum_(ell=1)^n u_(G_ell), independently of j.

At the end N_k of a run, the preceding run lengths divided by L_k tend to
zero. Therefore d[N_k,j]/N_k tends along alternate subsequences to u_A and
u_B. There is NO depth-average limit, despite all contacts being present,
uniform local mixing, and exact preservation of every temporal input word.

This disproves an inference from the local equations plus post-preparation
mixing to a common limiting speed for arbitrary depth arrangements. It does
NOT disprove the stronger conjecture restricted to states generated by the
specified learning protocol. Excluding this construction from the learned
class requires a property of learning across depth, not another timing-only
contraction estimate. Even uniform A and uniform B strips have different
device-level speeds, so universal speed across all admissible preparations
would need an additional selection mechanism.

6. WHAT A TRANSMISSIBLE LEARNED DOMAIN MUST CONTAIN

A reusable block contract needs a normalized date-shape domain, influential
gain bounds, physical parameter bounds, auxiliary modes and clocks, admissible
masked-port histories, recovery bounds, resource bounds, and enough information
to ensure the same physical event semantics at its entrance and exit.

For post-training propagation, gains can be fixed because physical protection
has been proved. For propagation OF TRAINING into fresh deeper cells, a single
future date vector and a gain box are not a sufficient state: the incoming
training history determines the new cells' learning. It must be represented
by a sound history contract or included in a sufficiently rich state. Reusing
a fitted terminal gain distribution is not a proof of learned-domain closure.

For heads whose gains vary between compared states, differentiation also gives

    dF/dg_i = -h(F-t_i)/S.

Thus the appropriate perturbation estimate contains a gain-forcing term,
for example e_next <= rho*e + K_g*eta. If a sound learning map additionally
gave eta_next <= L_h*e + kappa_g*eta, a sufficient joint contraction condition
would be kappa_g<1 and K_g*L_h < (1-rho)*(1-kappa_g). A weighted sum norm proves
this by choosing a weight w with rho+w*L_h<1 and kappa_g+K_g/w<1. These are
conditional inequalities, not measured coefficients of the current learner.

Protected future gains may simply retain their differences: then kappa_g=1.
Date mixing alone cannot be promoted to contraction across learned gain
profiles. Recovery b can remain dynamic without entering the head equation;
its task is to satisfy a uniform upper bound on protected duration and a
strict margin before the next relevant input. It need not converge if those
inequalities remain uniform.

7. PIPELINING: A NECESSARY CHANGE OF PROOF GEOMETRY

The finite proof uses a whole-network settling bound below 12.791 and input
gaps >=20. A positive-delay infinite strip cannot settle globally before each
new source pulse: different waves coexist at different depths.

The extension therefore needs a LOCAL, per-cell or finite-block regeneration
contract with wave identities. Same-port pulses must remain separated, all
cross-port inputs assigned to one wave must fit the admitted cohort, and each
cell must reset before the next wave reaches that cell. Global silence must
not be imposed at every source event. The construction in Section 5 satisfies
this by exact translation, local margins and a positive delay per layer.
For each finite time its excited causal cone has finite depth, so the infinite
strip is defined consistently by its finite prefixes; it does not introduce
an infinite cascade in finite time.

8. CONCRETE NEXT TARGET AND STOP CONDITIONS

The next proof target is a learned, recurrent block domain plus one justified
depth law: periodicity, convergence of the effective block map and drift, or
another independently proved condition giving convergence of the scalar
average. Choosing a convenient repeated block constructs an extension; it
does not prove that arbitrary independently trained layers select that block.

Before any long campaign:

* Propose a larger normalized domain containing both the layer-6 and layer-18
  shapes; do not reuse the disjoint stored rectangles as a self-map.
* Bound the three coupled quantities: head-date shape, learned gain/history
  state, and local recovery/cohort margins.
* Verify block image inclusion, effective influence overlap, and local
  pipeline regeneration with strict margins. A positive finite Q alone is
  not a pass for this step.
* Determine whether learning rules exclude alternating long depth runs with
  different scalar delays. If they do not, restrict the theorem explicitly
  rather than infer an average from simulations.
* Run the two-region interface proof attempt with fixed wiring and reachable
  states. A model with removed links is not the decisive physical test.

Failure categories must remain distinct: an invalid interval enclosure is
inconclusive; an admissible counterexample refutes the proposed claim; a
failure of training reachability leaves the post-preparation example outside
the learned theorem; an unproved infinite extension is an open obligation.
