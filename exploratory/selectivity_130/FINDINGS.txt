The learned configuration has a real filtering effect relative to saturation. For isolated full-amplitude lateral probes, the fraction reaching layer 130 is 9.79%, 4.38% and 6.04% at low, medium and high noise, versus 100% for both ungated-trained and uniform4 networks. Strict true recall is 99.323%, 99.876% and 99.863%; inclusive true recall is 99.336%, 99.945% and 99.945%. This is strong rejection of probes, but not exact equality of true-signal reliability.

The homogeneous median rule is not an adequate broadcast baseline on its own. Its weak lateral gains prevent the single-lane source from populating all 12 required output lanes. That is why the additional control retains the learned entrance before applying the simple rule in layers 7–130. This hybrid retains all learned b values as well and should not be described as a fully hand-designed replacement for learning.

The learned network does not generally outperform that stronger hybrid control. The hybrid gives zero false-only transmissions for the tested isolated lateral probes, while recovering all true signals. It also handles weakened central probes very well. These results do not support a universal claim that fine-grained learned gains are necessary for selectivity.

Mixed background noise exposes a context-dependent tradeoff. At low and medium noise, learned inclusive true recall is 99.408% and 99.961%, versus 98.438% and 99.297% for the hybrid. Paired95% intervals for these differences are [+0.111,+1.986] and [+0.241,+1.149] percentage points. However, learned produces on average 13.8 and 11.8 false-only output events per trial, versus zero for the hybrid. The hybrid tends to merge probe provenance into true outputs, while learned lets fewer probe identities through but sometimes produces separate false emissions. The two measures must not be conflated.

Noise-only tests also depend on what is counted. At low and medium noise, fewer injected probe identities reach the output with learned than with the hybrid, but successful learned false probes spread to more output lanes and create more false output events. At high noise the hybrid is better on both probe reach and false-event count. There is no single global winner across these endpoints and contamination regimes.

The energy picture changes when contamination is included. For full-amplitude lateral probes, learned total modeled work is 12.88%, 13.98% and 13.92% lower than uniform4 with the same b profile. In clean tests learned had slightly higher work. Thus filtering can offset the earlier clean-use overhead by preventing downstream processing of probes. These are total costs at the observed, not exactly equal, true-signal recall; they are model units, not measured joules.

The result supports functional selectivity of the learned configuration relative to saturation, with competitive simple alternatives and context-dependent compromises. Directional bias was already present in the initial gains (0.3,1.2,0.3), so this experiment does not demonstrate discovering direction from isotropic initialization. All 2562 datasets passed the raw-event and energy audits,183 clean historical regressions matched, and no injection-timing or solver-domain failures were hidden or discarded.
